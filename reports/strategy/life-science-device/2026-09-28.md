# Life Sciences Strategy Brief
## Week of September 28, 2026

## Executive View

- **FDA advisers accepted Galleri’s potential despite the absence of demonstrated population-level clinical utility.** The favorable benefit-risk vote materially improves the first multi-cancer early-detection test’s approval prospects, but the narrow effectiveness vote and failure of the 142,000-person NHS trial’s primary endpoint will complicate labeling, reimbursement and guideline adoption.
- **Obesity competition is expanding from maximum weight loss to maintenance and dosing flexibility.** Low-dose CagriSema delivered 21% weight loss, while Viking’s VK2735 maintained most prior loss with biweekly or monthly injections. Novo’s subsequent Nanexa licensing deal reinforces that dosing interval is becoming a strategic axis.
- **Acadia’s Alzheimer’s-disease-psychosis readout was a clinical miss, not a Phase 3 validation.** Remlifanserin failed its primary endpoint with a modest effect size; a positive secondary measure supports continued development but does not restore the program’s prior risk profile.
- **Autoimmune results emphasized disease and endpoint specificity.** Amgen’s CD40L antagonist succeeded in systemic Sjögren’s disease, while Immunovant discontinued cutaneous-lupus development of its FcRn antibody after a Phase 2 failure.
- **Once-weekly insulin is becoming a product class rather than a novelty.** FDA’s approval of Lilly’s Onswik offers substantial convenience, although glycemic efficacy was comparable—not superior—to daily basal insulin.

## Strategy Narrative

### Galleri crosses its regulatory hurdle without resolving whether multi-cancer screening improves outcomes

**What changed**

On September 23, FDA’s device advisory panel voted 7–2, with one abstention, that Galleri’s benefits outweigh its risks in adults aged 50 and older. Safety received unanimous support, but the effectiveness vote was only 6–4. FDA is not required to follow the recommendation. ([grail.gcs-web.com](https://grail.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fda-advisory-committee-votes-favor-approval-grails-gallerir?utm_source=openai))

The vote followed publication of two sharply contrasting datasets. In PATHFINDER 2, Galleri produced 60.3% positive predictive value, 99.64% specificity and one detected cancer per 185 people screened. However, the randomized 142,000-person NHS-Galleri trial found no reduction in stage III/IV cancers—the prespecified primary endpoint—after three annual screening rounds. ([nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04618-w?utm_source=openai))

**What it tells us**

Galleri can identify actionable cancer signals with relatively few false positives, but analytical and diagnostic performance are not equivalent to better population outcomes. The panel effectively accepted that detecting otherwise unscreened cancers may have sufficient prospective value to justify access before mortality or late-stage-disease benefits are established.

Approval would therefore represent a consequential regulatory precedent: MCED tests could enter clinical practice based on test performance, diagnostic yield and modeled benefit rather than proven mortality reduction. Commercial adoption will face a higher bar. Payers and guideline bodies can cite the randomized primary-endpoint failure when demanding postmarket evidence, coverage restrictions or registry-based implementation.

**What to watch next**

FDA’s final decision and permitted “early detection” claims; requirements to continue randomized follow-up; whether Galleri must remain additive to established single-cancer screening; and the coverage pathway that determines whether approval produces broad utilization or primarily self-pay demand.

### Obesity’s next battleground is maintenance architecture, not simply peak efficacy

**What changed**

Novo reported that CagriSema 1.0 mg/1.0 mg produced 21.0% mean weight loss versus 2.0% with placebo at 68 weeks. In patients with type 2 diabetes, the same dose achieved 12.4% loss versus 9.1% for tirzepatide 5 mg, with noninferior HbA1c reduction. The head-to-head result is encouraging but compares CagriSema with tirzepatide’s lowest maintenance dose, not its 10-mg or 15-mg efficacy ceiling. ([novonordisk.com](https://www.novonordisk.com/news-and-media/news-and-ir-materials/news-details.html?id=916774))

Viking separately reported 22% placebo-adjusted weight loss at week 33 in a weekly VK2735 cohort. After induction, patients transitioning to every-other-week dosing maintained up to 97% of prior loss over 12 weeks; monthly dosing maintained up to 90%, with maintenance-period gastrointestinal-event rates similar to placebo. These were exploratory, short-duration cohorts rather than a pivotal comparison. ([ir.vikingtherapeutics.com](https://ir.vikingtherapeutics.com/2026-09-22-Viking-Therapeutics-Announces-Positive-Topline-Results-from-Maintenance-Study-of-GLP-1-GIP-Agonist-VK2735-Demonstrating-the-Promise-of-Multiple-Maintenance-Dosing-Regimens?utm_source=openai))

Novo then licensed Nanexa’s depot-delivery technology for up to five peptide programs, targeting monthly and quarterly injections. The agreement carries potential payments of €1.165 billion, including upfront, development and regulatory milestones. ([prnewswire.com](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/nanexa-and-novo-enter-into-eur-1-165-billion-global-license-and-collaboration-agreement-for-long-acting-injectables-302890175.html))

**What it tells us**

The market is beginning to address a different unmet need: how to sustain chronic weight control with less treatment burden, lower drug consumption and potentially better persistence. CagriSema’s low-dose potency may provide titration flexibility, while VK2735’s pharmacokinetics could support a differentiated induction-to-maintenance regimen.

The evidence does not yet establish Viking as clinically superior or CagriSema as better than full-dose tirzepatide. It does show that long-acting delivery and maintenance dosing are becoming credible sources of differentiation alongside absolute weight loss. Novo’s capital-markets ambition to grow broadly in line with large-pharma peers—despite targeting more than five multi-blockbuster launches—also underscores how much execution risk remains in converting its pipeline into renewed outperformance. ([novonordisk.com](https://www.novonordisk.com/news-and-media/news-and-ir-materials/news-details.html?id=916773))

**What to watch next**

Longer VK2735 maintenance data, weight regain after missed doses, lean-mass preservation and Phase 3 tolerability; CagriSema’s expected fourth-quarter FDA decision; and whether monthly or quarterly depots can preserve efficacy without creating difficult titration or prolonged adverse-event exposure.

### Acadia’s Alzheimer’s-psychosis program now requires Phase 3 rescue rather than confirmation

**What changed**

Remlifanserin 60 mg reduced the SAPS hallucinations-and-delusions score by 12.6 points versus 10.4 with placebo at week six, missing the primary endpoint with an effect size of 0.26 and *p*=0.0603. The clinician-rated CGI-S-ADP secondary endpoint was nominally significant at *p*=0.0077, and Acadia reported favorable tolerability. The company plans to continue the ongoing Phase 3 program. ([sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1070494/000119312526401157/d168737dex991.htm))

**What it tells us**

The modest primary-endpoint separation cannot reasonably be characterized as clinical validation. The secondary result and apparent dose effect justify further study, but they may reflect an endpoint that is more responsive—or more subjective—rather than a robust antipsychotic effect.

Acadia must now optimize Phase 3 around placebo response, patient selection and endpoint sensitivity without appearing to redesign the program around a favorable secondary analysis. Given the company’s earlier regulatory setbacks in Alzheimer’s psychosis, FDA alignment will be especially important.

**What to watch next**

Full CTAD data in November, including the 30-mg arm, regional and baseline-severity subgroups, discontinuations and concordance across symptom measures; any Phase 3 protocol changes; and whether FDA accepts CGI-based evidence as supportive rather than primary.

### Autoimmune development is rewarding phenotype-specific mechanisms

Amgen’s approximately 621-patient OASIZ 301 trial met its week-48 ESSDAI primary endpoint for dazodalibep in moderate-to-severe systemic Sjögren’s disease, with improvement reported from week four. The result could support the first disease-modifying option for a population defined by systemic organ activity, but Amgen has not disclosed effect magnitude, responder rates or detailed safety. A separate Phase 3 study in patients with high symptom burden but low systemic activity is due to complete in the fourth quarter. ([investors.amgen.com](https://investors.amgen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/amgen-announces-positive-topline-phase-3-results-dazodalibep))

In contrast, Immunovant’s IMVT-1402 failed to significantly improve CLASI-A at week 12 in cutaneous lupus, prompting discontinuation in that indication. The result weakens the assumption that deep IgG reduction through FcRn blockade will translate broadly across autoantibody-associated diseases. ([immunovant.com](https://www.immunovant.com/investors/news-events/press-releases?utm_source=openai))

The emerging lesson is not that CD40L is superior to FcRn across autoimmunity. It is that mechanism, disease compartment and endpoint must align: interrupting T-cell/B-cell co-stimulation may be particularly relevant to systemic Sjögren’s, while skin-dominant lupus may not respond sufficiently to IgG lowering alone.

## FDA & Company Signals

- **Lilly’s Onswik was approved for once-weekly treatment of adults with type 2 diabetes.** Across four active-controlled studies, HbA1c reductions were similar to daily glargine or degludec. The label excludes type 1 diabetes because of severe-hypoglycemia risk. As the second U.S.-approved weekly insulin, its strategic value is convenience and potential improvement in insulin initiation and persistence—not superior glucose control. ([fda.gov](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-once-weekly-insulin-type-2-diabetes-mellitus?utm_source=openai))
- **Merck’s remigromig met the primary endpoint in pivotal diabetic-macular-edema testing.** The Wnt-pathway agonist provides initial late-stage validation for the EyeBio acquisition, but differentiation from entrenched anti-VEGF therapy will depend on disclosed safety, durability and injection frequency rather than noninferiority alone. ([merck.com](https://www.merck.com/news/mercks-remigromig-a-tri-specific-agonist-of-the-wingless-related-integration-site-wnt-pathway-met-primary-endpoint-in-the-pivotal-phase-2b-3-brunello-study-of-adults-with-diabetic-macular/?utm_source=openai))

## What I’m Watching Next

- Galleri’s final FDA decision, label and postmarket evidence obligations.
- Full VK2735 maintenance cohort sizes, durability and Phase 3 design.
- CagriSema’s FDA outcome and comparisons against therapeutic-dose tirzepatide.
- Remlifanserin’s CTAD presentation and any Phase 3 redesign.
- Dazodalibep’s absolute ESSDAI benefit, safety and symptomatic-Sjögren’s readout.
- Onswik pricing and adoption relative to Novo’s weekly insulin.

## Bottom Line

This week shifted competitive attention from whether technologies work to how regulators, clinicians and patients will use them. Galleri may reach the market despite unresolved clinical utility; obesity developers are designing induction and maintenance ecosystems rather than simply chasing the largest weight-loss number; and weekly insulin is becoming a convenience class.

The counterexamples were equally instructive. Acadia’s primary miss shows that a favorable secondary endpoint cannot substitute for robust efficacy, while the divergence between Sjögren’s and cutaneous-lupus programs reinforces that broad immunological rationales still require disease-specific clinical validation.
