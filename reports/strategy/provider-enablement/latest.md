# Provider Enablement Strategy Brief
## Week of November 16, 2026

## Executive View
- CMS raised the Medicare Shared Savings Program’s BASIC Level E sharing rate from 50% to 60% and gave January 2027 applicants until November 17 to reconsider their risk-track selection. ([CMS, *CY 2027 PFS Final Rule — Medicare Shared Savings Program*, November 4, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/calendar-year-cy-2027-medicare-physician-fee-schedule-final-rule-cms-1848-f-medicare-shared-savings))
- A new ACO-specific modifier will add 32% to qualifying evaluation-and-management payments, versus 16% outside MSSP and LEAD, improving practice cash flow beginning January 1, 2027. ([CMS, *CY 2027 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule Final Rule*, November 4, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/calendar-year-cy-2027-medicare-physician-fee-schedule-final-rule))
- CMS initiated payment-year 2023 RADV audits using models that target enrollees expected to have the largest risk-score reductions, raising medical-record and coding-liability exposure for MA plans and delegated providers. ([CMS, *Payment Year 2023 MA Contract-Specific RADV Audit Methods and Instructions*, November 13, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/radv-py2023-audit-methods-instructions.pdf))
- Missouri selected Molina to replace UnitedHealthcare in its next Medicaid managed-care cycle, requiring a 2027 payer and data transition across a program with approximately one million members. ([Missouri Hospital Association, *MO HealthNet Selects Managed Care Plans*, November 12, 2026](https://www.mohospitals.org/newsroom/mo-healthnet-selects-managed-care-plans/))
- Overall environment: broadly unchanged because improved Medicare ACO economics are offset by greater MA audit exposure and Medicaid payer-partner displacement.

## 1. CMS improves ACO upside and gives 2027 applicants six days to reconsider risk
**Status:** NEW

**What happened**

CMS published the 2027 Physician Fee Schedule final rule on November 9 and opened a November 12-17 window for January 2027 MSSP applicants to change their final risk-track selection. BASIC Level E’s shared-savings rate rises from 50% to 60%, while the maximum positive regional adjustment weight for lower-spending ENHANCED ACOs falls from 50% to 35%. CMS also increased the prior-savings adjustment factor from 50% to 75% and risk-adjusted the 5% cap on upward benchmark adjustments. ([CMS, *CY 2027 PFS Final Rule — Medicare Shared Savings Program*, November 4, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/calendar-year-cy-2027-medicare-physician-fee-schedule-final-rule-cms-1848-f-medicare-shared-savings); [CMS, *CMS-1848-F*, November 9, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/fee-schedules/physician/federal-regulation-notices/cms-1848-f))

The rule also creates modifier B2, adding 32% to qualifying E/M payments for MSSP and LEAD clinicians, compared with 16% under modifier B1. B2 may be billed for all patients treated by eligible clinicians, although the claims enter assignment, benchmark and performance-expenditure calculations. ([CMS, *CY 2027 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule Final Rule*, November 4, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/calendar-year-cy-2027-medicare-physician-fee-schedule-final-rule))

**Implication**

BASIC Level E and physician-led ACOs gain both shared-savings upside and immediate practice cash flow. ENHANCED ACOs dependent on favorable regional benchmarks lose relative value. Demand rises immediately for track-selection modeling, provider compensation design and benchmark analysis.

**Watch next**

Whether applicants switch from ENHANCED to BASIC Level E before the November 17 deadline.

## 2. CMS starts 2023 RADV audits before resolving extrapolated collections
**Status:** NEW

**What happened**

CMS notified MA organizations selected for payment-year 2023 contract-specific RADV audits on November 13. The sampling frame uses diagnoses from 2022 and targets enrollees ranked in the top quartile by one or both of CMS’s improper-payment prediction models. Depending on contract size, CMS will audit 35, 50, 100 or 200 enrollees. Enrollee data lists become available November 30, and medical-record submissions run from December 4, 2026, through April 23, 2027. ([CMS, *Payment Year 2023 MA Contract-Specific RADV Audit Methods and Instructions*, November 13, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/radv-py2023-audit-methods-instructions.pdf))

CMS designed the audits to calculate extrapolated overpayments but has not decided whether it will collect those amounts or limit recoveries to errors found in sampled enrollees. The 2023 extrapolation rule was vacated in 2025, and the ruling remains under appeal. ([CMS, *Payment Year 2023 MA Contract-Specific RADV Audit Methods and Instructions*, November 13, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/radv-py2023-audit-methods-instructions.pdf))

**Implication**

The immediate cost is operational: plans must retrieve four-year-old records and will push documentation requests into provider networks. The financial exposure depends on extrapolation and on whether delegated-risk contracts pass coding liabilities to provider organizations. Enablement demand shifts toward source-record validation, retrieval workflow, audit support and contract-level liability analysis.

**Watch next**

The November 30 enrollee lists, plan disclosures and any appellate decision clarifying extrapolation authority.

## 3. Missouri’s Medicaid award recommendation replaces UnitedHealthcare with Molina
**Status:** NEW

**What happened**

Missouri released award recommendations on November 10 that retain Centene’s Home State Health and Elevance’s Healthy Blue while adding Molina as a new general-plan contractor. UnitedHealthcare and Aetna were not selected. Home State also retained the sole specialty-plan award; Molina’s and UnitedHealthcare’s specialty proposals were deemed nonresponsive because of professional-licensure requirements. The new contracts are scheduled to begin July 1, 2027. ([Health Management Associates, *Weekly Roundup*, November 11, 2026](https://www.healthmanagement.com/insights/weekly-roundup-v2/november-11-2026/); [Missouri Hospital Association, *MO HealthNet Selects Managed Care Plans*, November 12, 2026](https://www.mohospitals.org/newsroom/mo-healthnet-selects-managed-care-plans/))

Missouri reported 1.015 million managed-care members in August. Summing the four regions, UnitedHealthcare served approximately 312,000, although future member allocation among the winning plans has not been disclosed. ([Missouri Department of Social Services, *MO HealthNet Managed Care Enrollment, SFY 2027*, August 28, 2026](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/pdf/2026/08/SFY-2027-Managed-Care-Enrollment-Monthly-2026-08-28_AOD.pdf))

**Implication**

Missouri preserves capitated managed care but redistributes the payer relationships funding provider risk. UnitedHealthcare-aligned groups face contract, attribution and care-management-data transitions; Molina gains a statewide entry point for alternative payment arrangements. The transition creates a near-term displacement opportunity for enablement vendors that can migrate eligibility, claims, quality and risk data without interrupting provider cash flow.

**Watch next**

Procurement protests, final contracts, member-assignment rules and continuity-of-care requirements.

## Bottom Line
CMS’s combined improvement to MSSP shared-savings terms and ACO physician payments is the week’s most important change because it directly strengthens provider cash flow and two-sided-risk participation economics for 2027. MA coding defensibility needs the closest attention as the payment-year 2023 RADV cycle reaches provider records. The November 17 MSSP selection deadline is the next event most likely to change enablement demand.
