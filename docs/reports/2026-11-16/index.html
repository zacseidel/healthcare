1. CMS turns first-half 2027 Medicaid work checks into a shadow eligibility period

What happened

States may suspend Medicaid eligibility denials and disenrollments tied solely to community-engagement noncompliance through June 30, 2027. They must still assess qualifying activities, deemed compliance and exclusions for relevant applications and renewals beginning January 1. CMS will exclude cases handled under an approved mitigation plan from PERM, Medicaid Eligibility Quality Control and other beneficiary-eligibility audit errors. States seeking the option from January 1 should notify CMS by December 11. (CMS, Optional Hold Harmless Period for Community Engagement Implementation, November 13, 2026)

Implication

The policy defers the first wave of work-related coverage losses, supporting Medicaid MCO membership and reducing near-term uncompensated-care exposure for providers. It does not reduce implementation scope. States still need data matching, exclusion logic, notices, caseworker workflows and auditable eligibility decisions.

January through June becomes a production testing period in states adopting the option. Eligibility vendors gain additional time to correct system defects, while MCOs and providers retain more opportunity to intervene before adverse determinations. Variation will remain high because states can decline the option or use it for a shorter period.

Watch next

State mitigation-plan decisions by December 11 and whether CMS litigation or additional guidance changes the July 1 enforcement threshold.

2. A rival bid raises the price and closing obligations for Surgery Partners

What happened

HealthSpan Partners increased its offer for Surgery Partners to $25 per share, lifting enterprise value from approximately $5.7 billion to as much as $6.0 billion after Surgery Partners received an unsolicited competing proposal. The amendment adds a 10% annualized ticking fee after March 1, 2027, capped at $1.61 per share, and removes the materiality limit from HealthSpan’s obligation to take actions needed to resolve antitrust impediments. The termination fee increased to approximately $149.1 million. (SEC, Surgery Partners Form 8-K, November 9, 2026)

Surgery Partners also reported 6.0% same-facility revenue growth, driven by 5.8% higher revenue per case and only 0.2% case growth. (SEC, Surgery Partners Third Quarter 2026 Results, November 9, 2026)

Implication

The amendment strengthens last week’s thesis that scaled ASC networks have become scarce strategic assets for health systems. Competing interest raised the purchase price despite limited underlying volume growth, indicating that geographic reach, physician relationships and site-of-care positioning carry substantial option value.

The expanded regulatory covenant improves closing certainty but may require HealthSpan to accept divestitures or operating restrictions. Payers should assess concentration and reimbursement exposure in markets where Surgery Partners overlaps with Bon Secours Mercy Health.

Watch next

The identity of the rival bidder, the proxy filing and any antitrust remedy affecting local ASC ownership or contracting.

3. CMS extends the home-health and hospice entry freeze while letting incumbents add locations

What happened

CMS extended nationwide moratoria on initial Medicare enrollment of home-health agencies and hospices, including certain changes in majority ownership, for another six months. Unlike the May moratoria, currently enrolled organizations may now add practice locations. CMS said an October California operation produced 145 revocations involving providers associated with more than $703 million in Medicare and Medicaid payments. (CMS, CMS Extends and Modifies Nationwide Freeze on New Medicare Hospice and Home Health Enrollment, November 12, 2026; Federal Register, Extension and Modification of the Nationwide Temporary Moratorium on Enrollment of Hospices, November 16, 2026)

Implication

The extension adds a market-structure effect to last week’s retroactive-revocation policy. Greenfield Medicare entry and some control transactions remain blocked, while established operators can expand through additional locations. Scaled incumbents therefore gain a six-month advantage in recruiting clinicians, entering adjacent service areas and negotiating payer relationships.

The ownership restriction may also complicate acquisitions structured around new billing enrollments or majority-control changes. Diligence must address enrollment continuity, managing personnel and whether a transaction falls within the moratorium rather than treating provider numbers as routine closing items.

Watch next

Access indicators in rural markets, further payment suspensions and whether CMS extends or narrows the moratoria again in May 2027.

4. Federal prior-authorization testing shifts the risk from APIs to end-to-end workflow

What happened

ONC and CMS launched voluntary 10- to 12-week testing sprints for payers, providers, EHR developers and intermediaries using production-like environments. Testing will cover the Da Vinci Coverage Requirements Discovery, Documentation Templates and Rules, and Prior Authorization Support implementation guides. MITRE will support recruitment and coordination, while Drummond Group will operate the FHIRplace testing environment. Organizations must express interest by November 20; participation does not constitute certification or a CMS compliance determination. (ONC, ONC and CMS Launch Industry Electronic Prior Authorization Testing Initiative, November 12, 2026)

Implication

The initiative recognizes that standards conformance does not ensure an operational authorization. The remaining failure points sit between organizations: coverage-rule discovery, clinical-document generation, identity matching, attachments, exception handling and status reconciliation.

That increases demand for workflow orchestration, test data, monitoring and implementation services around payer and EHR APIs. Clearinghouses and interoperability vendors can capture work that neither payer cores nor EHRs manage alone. Voluntary testing may reduce deployment risk, but organizations remain responsible for compliance and cannot treat participation as a safe harbor.

Watch next

The first-round participants, selected clinical use cases and whether sprint findings become formal implementation guidance or change enforcement expectations.

5. P3’s margin recovery still leaves a financing problem

What happened

P3 Health reported third-quarter adjusted EBITDA of $20.9 million versus a $45.9 million loss a year earlier. Medical margin reached $43.9 million, or $140 per member per month, but fell to $35.0 million and $111 PMPM after excluding payer settlements and prior-year development. At-risk membership declined 9% to approximately 105,000 following network and payer rationalization. (SEC, P3 Health Partners Third Quarter 2026 Results, November 9, 2026)

P3 nevertheless reported $43.9 million of unrestricted cash, a $190.6 million working-capital deficit and $90.1 million of nine-month operating cash use, concluding that substantial doubt remains about its ability to continue as a going concern. (SEC, P3 Health Partners Form 10-Q, November 9, 2026)

Implication

The result strengthens the case that selective payer and provider contraction can repair value-based-care unit economics. It weakens the assumption that positive adjusted EBITDA resolves the sector’s capital problem. Settlement timing, preferred obligations, debt maturities and claims funding can still overwhelm reported operating improvement.

Health-plan and physician partners should treat liquidity as a service-continuity and counterparty risk. Smaller enablement platforms may need refinancing, asset sales or strategic capital before their improved medical margins convert into sustainable cash generation.

Watch next

P3’s capital-raising or refinancing plan and recurring 2027 medical margin after prior-period benefits fall away.

What to Watch For

  • November 20, 2026: Organizations must express interest in the first ONC-CMS electronic prior-authorization testing round. (ONC, ONC and CMS Launch Industry Electronic Prior Authorization Testing Initiative, November 12, 2026)
  • December 7, 2026: Medicare annual enrollment ends, providing the first firm membership read after 2027 benefit and Stars changes. (CMS, Medicare Advantage and Medicare Prescription Drug Programs Expected to Remain Stable in 2027, October 2026)
  • December 11, 2026: States intending to use the Medicaid work-requirement hold-harmless option from January 1 are asked to notify CMS. (CMS, Optional Hold Harmless Period for Community Engagement Implementation, November 13, 2026)
  • December 15, 2026: Comments close on CMS’s proposed MA provider-directory and Part D PBM accountability rule. (Federal Register, CMS-4214-P, October 20, 2026)

Bottom Line

CMS’s Medicaid hold-harmless option is the week’s most important economic change because it defers first-half 2027 coverage losses while preserving the full eligibility-technology build. State implementation choices and resulting MCO membership variation need the closest attention. The December 11 notification date is the next event most likely to determine how much coverage and provider-cash exposure moves from January into the second half of 2027.

Hims & Hers (HIMS) -29.9% Last 3m

Its offerings include generic, branded, and compounded prescription drugs as well as over-the-counter medicines, cosmetics, and supplements. The platform, which has more than 2 million subscribers, is available in all 50 states and certain European markets like the UK. It includes provider networks, electronic medical records, cloud pharmacy fulfillment, and personalization capabilities. Hims does not take insurance and only accepts payments directly from customers.
