Week of September 7, 2026Market data through: September 4, 2026Narrative created: September 7, 2026

Weekly review of pharmaceutical, biotech, biologics, and medical-device company performance, changes, earnings activity, and strategy narrative.

In the News

Executive View

  • The first large cardiovascular-outcomes test of Lp(a) lowering failed. Pelacarsen reduced the biomarker but not cardiovascular events, weakening confidence that Lp(a) reduction alone will translate into benefit and materially raising the evidentiary bar for Amgen’s olpasiran and Lilly’s lepodisiran. (novartis.com)
  • Fatal inflammatory events reset the risk assessment for autoimmune CAR-T. Novartis paused eight rap-cel studies after three deaths from complications of IEC-HS; Bristol Myers Squibb separately paused zola-cel enrollment following inflammatory events. (lupus.org)
  • FDA institutionalized treatment switching before radiographic progression. Camizestrant became the first cancer therapy approved for use when serial circulating-tumor-DNA testing detects an acquired resistance mutation before imaging shows progression. (fda.gov)
  • Two bispecifics strengthened the case that implementation can be as differentiating as efficacy. AbbVie’s monthly etentamig delivered strong pivotal myeloma results with predominantly low-grade cytokine-release syndrome, while ivonescimab achieved an overall-survival advantage over Keytruda in a China-only lung-cancer study. (news.abbvie.com)
  • Neurological antisense therapy produced a sharp success/failure contrast. Ionis won the first Alexander-disease approval as Ultragenyx’s Angelman program failed unequivocally in Phase 3, reinforcing that target engagement and encouraging uncontrolled data cannot substitute for reproducible functional benefit. (fda.gov)

Strategy Narrative

Pelacarsen turns Lp(a) from a biomarker race into an outcomes problem

What changed

Novartis reported on September 4 that the 8,323-patient Phase 3 Lp(a)HORIZON trial failed its primary four-component cardiovascular endpoint despite lowering Lp(a). Participants had established cardiovascular disease and were already receiving guideline-directed lipid-lowering and antihypertensive therapy. Effect sizes, achieved Lp(a) reductions and component endpoints remain undisclosed. (novartis.com)

What it tells us

This does not conclusively refute Lp(a) biology: pelacarsen’s degree, consistency and duration of reduction may matter, as may baseline Lp(a), residual risk and endpoint composition. It does, however, refute the assumption that lowering the biomarker with any effective oligonucleotide will necessarily reduce events.

Commercial expectations for the entire category should therefore be rebased around outcomes rather than biomarker potency. Amgen and Lilly are testing siRNAs capable of deeper, more durable Lp(a) suppression, but their ongoing Phase 3 programs now carry more biological—not merely execution—risk. (investors.amgen.com)

What to watch next

The full Lp(a)HORIZON data, particularly achieved reduction, treatment duration, high-Lp(a) subgroups and individual cardiovascular-event components.

Autoimmune CAR-T’s benefit-risk threshold moved materially higher

What changed

Novartis halted screening, randomization and dosing across eight autoimmune and neurological rapcabtagene-autoleucel studies after three participants developed serious immune effector cell-associated hemophagocytic syndrome and subsequently died. Bristol Myers separately paused autoimmune studies of zolacabtagene autoleucel after detecting inflammatory events described as transient and reversible. (lupus.org)

What it tells us

The therapeutic thesis—deep B-cell depletion producing durable, potentially drug-free remission—remains compelling. But severe autoimmune disease generally offers less tolerance for treatment-related mortality than refractory cancer. A safety profile acceptable in oncology may therefore be commercially and regulatorily unacceptable in lupus, multiple sclerosis or myasthenia gravis.

These events do not establish a class-wide toxicity. They will nevertheless increase scrutiny of cell-expansion kinetics, dose, lymphodepletion, patient selection, inflammatory biomarkers and whether rheumatology or neurology sites can recognize and manage delayed immune complications.

What to watch next

Whether the events cluster by indication, protocol or manufacturing design; regulatory requirements for restarting the studies; and whether competing programs alter dosing or monitoring proactively.

Camizestrant makes serial liquid biopsy part of treatment—not merely diagnosis

What changed

FDA granted accelerated approval to AstraZeneca’s oral SERD camizestrant with a continuing CDK4/6 inhibitor when Guardant360 CDx detects an emerging ESR1 mutation during aromatase-inhibitor/CDK4/6 therapy, before radiographic progression. In the 315-patient SERENA-6 trial, median progression-free survival was 16.0 months versus 9.2 months for continued aromatase inhibition, a 56% reduction in progression or death risk. (fda.gov)

What it tells us

The strategic advance is broader than another endocrine drug. FDA has endorsed longitudinal molecular surveillance as a trigger for changing treatment while the patient remains clinically and radiographically stable. That creates recurring diagnostic demand and could extend first-line CDK4/6 use while displacing some post-progression therapy.

The unresolved question is whether earlier switching improves survival or primarily shifts the treatment clock forward, exposing patients to a new drug—and payers to additional cost—sooner.

What to watch next

Overall-survival maturation, confirmatory requirements, adherence to serial ctDNA testing and payer policies governing testing frequency.

Etentamig and ivonescimab pressure incumbents through different forms of differentiation

AbbVie’s Phase 3 CERVINO trial showed a 74.0% response rate for BCMA×CD3 bispecific etentamig versus 45.7% with standard regimens, with a PFS hazard ratio of 0.40. Grade 3/4 infections remained substantial at 27.7%, but cytokine-release syndrome was predominantly grade 1, with no grade 3 or higher events reported. Monthly dosing began after a single step-up dose. (news.abbvie.com)

The value proposition is therefore not simply another active BCMA therapy. A manageable initiation process and monthly schedule could move treatment beyond specialized centers, expanding the economically reachable market even without demonstrating superior efficacy to another BCMA bispecific.

Separately, ivonescimab monotherapy achieved statistically significant overall survival versus pembrolizumab in PD-L1-positive first-line NSCLC in China. Coming one week after its biliary-tract survival win, the result further strengthens the PD-1/VEGF architecture; however, undisclosed effect sizes and the China-only population leave global differentiation unresolved. Summit’s U.S. decision for a different EGFR-mutant indication is due November 14. (smmttx.com)

Additional Strategic Signals

  • Rare neurological ASOs diverged on functional evidence. FDA approved Ionis’s quarterly intrathecal zilganersen after a 49-patient controlled study demonstrated improved walking speed at 61 weeks in eligible patients with Alexander disease. By contrast, Ultragenyx found no supportive efficacy signal on cognition, its multidomain responder index or any component measure in Phase 3 Angelman syndrome. The Ionis opportunity is commercially constrained by extreme rarity, but scientifically validates GFAP reduction and Ionis’s independent-launch model. (fda.gov)
  • Voluntary MFN agreements are becoming negotiated policy safe harbors. Nine additional manufacturers joined the administration’s pricing framework, extending agreements to companies representing a reported 89% of the branded market. Commitments include Medicaid MFN access, future-launch pricing and $19.6 billion in collective U.S. manufacturing investment. BridgeBio expects orphan-only products to be excluded and anticipates protection from future pricing mandates, although its SEC filing explicitly cautions that those interpretations may change. (whitehouse.gov)
  • Medtronic’s earnings altered the robotics thesis more than the financial beat did. The company paired strong cardiac-ablation growth with a new Cornerstone Robotics partnership and FDA clearance of its next-generation Touch Surgery Aide platform. Its 13.7% quarterly organic growth should not be extrapolated—the extra selling week contributed approximately $570 million—but Medtronic is assembling a broader robotics, AI and instrumentation portfolio rather than relying solely on Hugo. (news.medtronic.com)

What I’m Watching Next

  • Full Lp(a)HORIZON data and responses from competing siRNA developers.
  • Regulatory attribution and restart conditions for autoimmune CAR-T studies.
  • Overall-survival evidence supporting camizestrant’s pre-progression treatment switch.
  • Complete HARMONi-2 and etentamig safety, efficacy and administration data.
  • Ultragenyx’s disposition of apazunersen and resulting expense reductions.

Bottom Line

This week challenged the assumption that compelling biology reliably becomes clinical value. Lp(a) lowering failed its first major outcomes test; an Angelman ASO failed despite promising earlier evidence; and autoimmune CAR-T encountered a potentially modality-defining safety problem. The counterexamples were equally instructive: camizestrant coupled molecular surveillance to a large PFS gain, etentamig combined efficacy with deployability, and zilganersen converted target reduction into measurable function.

The strategic premium is shifting toward therapies that prove not only that they engage the intended biology, but that doing so improves outcomes safely, reproducibly and within a workable care pathway.

Medtronic (MDT) +15.3%

Reported: September 1, 2026 · Google Finance earnings page

Earnings Call Summary

Medtronic delivered a strong start to fiscal 2027, reporting Q1 revenue of $9.8 billion and adjusted EPS of $1.45, both exceeding company expectations. Organic revenue growth reached 13.7%, boosted by approximately 670 basis points from an extra selling week. Major segments including CRM (+15%), CST (+13%), and Surgical (+9%) all showed strong underlying market demand.

At a Glance

  • Medtronic Beats Q1 Earnings Estimates: Medtronic reported a quarterly revenue of $9.756 billion and an adjusted EPS of $1.14, outperforming the estimated revenue of $9.545 billion.
  • Strong Cardiovascular and Portfolio Performance: The company delivered 13.7% organic revenue growth, heavily supported by an 88% surge in its Cardiac Ablation Solutions business and broad strength across major segments.
  • Full Year Financial Guidance Raised: Following robust quarterly performance, management raised its fiscal year 2027 organic revenue growth guidance to 7.25%–7.75% and boosted adjusted EPS guidance to a range of $5.94 to $6.00.
  • Expanded Footprint in Robotic Surgery: Medtronic disclosed a $700 million strategic partnership and distribution agreement with Cornerstone Robotics for its Sentire surgical system in select international markets.
  • Extra Selling Week Drives Growth: The headline financial performance for the first quarter included an extra fiscal selling week, which contributed approximately $570 million to the overall organic revenue figures.

Key Moments from the Call

  • 28m 21s — Strong Q1 Outperformance and Raised Guidance: Medtronic exceeded Q1 consensus expectations on both top and bottom lines, leading management to lift full-year fiscal 2027 organic revenue growth guidance by 50 basis points to 7.25%–7.75%. This revision reflects strong broad-based procedure volumes, robust underlying market demand, and successful execution across key growth franchises.
  • 38m 23s — Strategic Expansion via Cornerstone Investment: The company announced a $700 million strategic investment and distribution deal for Cornerstone Robotics' Sentire system. The transaction pleases investors looking for enhanced scale in soft-tissue robotics but triggered questions regarding the performance of the core Hugo platform, which management actively defended as growing at twice the market rate.
  • 51m 5s — Robust Cardiac Ablation Momentum: The Cardiac Ablation Solutions business grew 88% globally and is projected to outpace market growth by more than 3x in Q2. Investors will be encouraged by the 35% sequential expansion in the U.S. Affera installed base, which ensures long-term, high-margin catheter pull-through that will eventually improve mixed-gross margin pressures.
  • 56m 53s — Unchanged MiniMed Separation Strategy: Management reiterated its commitment to the separation of the MiniMed diabetes business prior to fiscal year-end, pointing to its accelerating U.S. growth (+15% in Q1). Deconsolidation is expected to expand enterprise gross margin by 50 basis points and operating margin by 100 basis points, optimizing capital allocation toward higher-return segments.

BridgeBio Pharma is a biotechnology company focused on discovering, developing, testing, and delivering transformative treatments for patients with genetic diseases. The company has four programs in its late-stage pipeline focusing on Mendelian disorders, oncology, and gene therapy. One of its key programs, Attruby (acoramidis), is an orally administered small molecule designed to stabilize tetrameric transthyretin for the treatment of transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy.
