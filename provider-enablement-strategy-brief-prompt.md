## Delta-first requirement

This is a weekly change report, not a recurring overview of VBC or MA/Medicaid risk contracting. Review the supplied previous Monday briefing and treat it as the baseline of what the reader already knows. Older prior-report digests are for hypothesis tracking only. Do not treat intra-week notes as last week's published report.

The central question is:

> What did we learn this week that materially changes, strengthens, weakens, resolves, or extends our existing understanding of MA/Medicaid risk-contracting economics, at-risk provider economics, or the VBC-enablement demand that sits on those economics?

Do not repeat a development, regulation, Stars or RAF setup, payer-partnership structure, company thesis, or strategic conclusion merely because it remains important. A reader who read last week's briefing should not encounter substantial passages that could have appeared essentially unchanged the week before. It is preferable to produce a shorter briefing during a quiet week than to fill space with old material.

## Inclusion test

A development should generally appear only if it qualifies as at least one of:

- **NEW:** A genuinely new development, data print, policy action, company disclosure, competitive move, contract change, or strategic hypothesis emerged.
- **UPDATE:** New information materially changes the magnitude, timing, probability, economics, or strategic significance of something previously discussed.
- **REFUTE:** New evidence materially weakens or contradicts an existing hypothesis.
- **RESOLVE:** New information answers an important question or catalyst identified previously.

Mere consistency with an existing thesis is insufficient. **CONFIRM is not a standalone section.** If new evidence only strengthens a standing hypothesis, mention it in one Executive View bullet or omit it.

In-window watchlist earnings and large movers are a scan list, not optional color. Include a name when the print changes risk-contract mix, attributed-life growth, payer-partnership terms, or enablement-platform competitive position. Omit only after that judgment. A beat-and-raise with no thesis change is still omit.

Recurring program awards or renewals of a structure already covered (for example another state's MCO procurement using terms already discussed) are Watch-next items unless the contract terms or funded capabilities change the demand thesis.

Before including something, ask:

> If I removed this item, would the reader lose consequential information that became known, materially changed, or became meaningfully more certain during the last seven days?

If not, omit it. Do not write stub sections for payers, providers, CMS, companies, or the calendar when nothing material moved.

## Analysis expected for each major development

Do not merely summarize articles. For each consequential development, work through:

**What happened -> Implication -> What to watch next**

**What happened** is facts only. Put the commercial takeaway in **Implication**, not twice. Inside the implication, assess the first affected market or administrative process, the transmission to MA/Medicaid risk-contract economics, at-risk provider cash/operations, or VBC-enablement platform competition, the exposure affected, directional implications, likely magnitude, time horizon, and confidence. Distinguish confirmed facts, estimates, management opinions, advocacy, inference and scenarios. Rank developments by likely effect on those economics, not by headline volume.

Typical transmission paths, when they apply:

- MA rate, RAF, coding-intensity, or RADV change → at-risk provider bid/contract economics and chart-review / in-home-assessment / risk-adjustment vendor demand
- Stars or HEDIS methodology change → quality-bonus revenue under risk contracts and care-gap / quality-tech spend
- Medical-cost trend or utilization surprise → UM tooling intensity and shared-risk/full-risk provider margin
- Payer-VBC partnership expansion, restriction, or termination → enablement-platform attributed-life growth and switching costs
- CMMI model change (ACO REACH, MSSP, state VBC models) → provider participation economics and enablement demand
- Denial, underpayment, or audit action → at-risk provider cash conversion
- Labor, capacity, or site-of-care shift → at-risk provider margins and automation/ambulatory demand
- Vertical integration or antitrust action among payers and primary-care aggregators → who captures risk-contract surplus

When relevant, identify whether the development creates a new hypothesis, strengthens an existing one, weakens or revises one, or resolves a previously identified uncertainty.

For continuing stories:

1. One sentence of prior thesis.
2. Lead with the new information.
3. Explain how it changes the prior view.
4. Explain the incremental implication.
5. Identify the next catalyst or evidence that matters.

If the implication sentence could have appeared last week, omit the story. Do not repeat the entire background narrative. Do not infer causation from equity-price movement alone, fabricate unavailable values, or overstate importance. State "reliable public value unavailable" rather than inventing a medical-cost trend, RAF coefficient, Stars cutoff, risk-contract margin, or attributed-life count.
